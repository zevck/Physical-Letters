# The courier on the road

When a letter is on its way along the road the player is travelling, the player may meet the courier carrying it. He can be asked to post a letter, threatened into handing over his letters, brawled for them, or pickpocketed. Letters he still has when he's gone go on their way; letters he hasn't, the player took. Code: `src/RoadCourier.cpp` (detection, the natives, the cooldown) and `src/Transit.cpp` (the letters); records in [PLUGIN.md](PLUGIN.md#records); settings `[Courier] RoadEncounters` and `RoadCooldownDays` in [SETTINGS.md](SETTINGS.md). The courier is the vanilla one, as in town ([COURIER.md](COURIER.md#why-the-vanilla-courier)).

## How vanilla meets travellers on the road

Vanilla's own mechanism, documented in `WETriggerScript.psc`: trigger boxes placed along the roads send the story event `WERoadStart` (keyword `0x1027A6`) when the player walks in, with the trigger as `Ref1`. Each trigger is linked to a pair of `WETravel` markers (location reference type `0x04A5F5`) "arranged such that ... a creature spawned at one of the markers traveling to the other will intercept" the player. The Story Manager branch `WERoadQuests` (`0x1027A7`, under `WEQuestNode` `0x0896A8`, which needs `WEEnabled`) picks one road encounter at random; it creates its travellers at one marker with a plain Travel package to somewhere far, and the navmesh's preferred paths keep them on the road. The trigger switches itself off when it fires; the encounter's shutdown stage calls `WETriggerScript.ReArmTrigger()`.

## Is a letter passing?

Every letter on its way has a **route**, set when it sets out: the straight line between where it left (whoever held it, the writer, or a dead recipient for a returned letter) and where it goes (the recipient, or the player for a letter to them), on the exterior map (`Travel::PointOf`: an interior counts as its location's marker), and when it set out (a reply, once written: `WritingHours` after the reading). A letter whose two ends are on different maps has none.

Every heartbeat `RoadCourier::Tick` checks the letters in transit (to an NPC, or to the courier for the player) against the player's position, outdoors (no town check: a town's location reaches far past its walls, Whiterun's over most of the road to Riverwood, which hid the letter for a whole run; vanilla's road triggers are only on roads anyway):

- the route is at least 16,384 units long (shorter is within one town);
- it passes within **16,384 units** of the player (about four exterior cells);
- the letter has set out and isn't due yet.

**No timing check.** A first version also wanted the letter about as far along as the player (its share of the travel time within 0.25 of the player's point along the route). Letters travel at walking pace (Riverwood to Whiterun: 2 game hours, about 6 real minutes) and the player runs about three times as fast, so the window lasted a minute or so and no road trigger fell inside it (two runs, no courier). Meeting him a little early or late on the letter's road is fine; nobody times the courier.

Roads curve, but between two towns they follow the line closely enough for "roughly in the courier's path". The global `PhysicalLettersRoadCourierReady` is 1 while a letter passes, encounters are on and the cooldown (`RoadCooldownDays`, 3) is over; the log says when it changes (debug).

## An encounter

1. **The trigger**, only in business hours (8:00 to 20:00, `GetCurrentTime` on the node). Our Story Manager node, `PhysicalLettersRoadCourierNode`, sits under `WEQuestNode` next to `WERoadQuests` (it names the same previous sibling, `0x0F2199`, so it should come first: to confirm in game). It needs `WERoadStart`, the global, and the courier in his holding cell (so not on an errand in town or delivering mail). It doesn't share the event: when he comes, he replaces vanilla's encounter for that trigger.
2. **The letters.** The quest `PLRoadCourier` asks the DLL (`TakeLetters`): the letters passing here, **nearest first, and only those going the same way as the nearest** (routes within 30°), go into his inventory (parcel states 5 and 6, from in transit and to the player). So Solitude→Riften and Morthal→Riften letters can ride together where their roads meet; a letter the other way can't. None, and the quest re-arms the trigger and stops.
3. **Where he goes.** The trigger's two travel markers fill as in vanilla's road quests; he's moved to the one farther from the player during the trigger (out of sight) and travels (`PhysicalLettersRoadCourierTravel`, the Travel template, jogging: the courier is always on the move) to his **destination**: the map marker of the recipient's town (`Destination`), or for a letter to the player, whichever travel marker is further along the letter's route (`Downstream`), so he always heads the way the letter goes. The cooldown starts.
4. **His phases** (quest stages; his packages, first match wins: rest, walk on, jog):
   - **10/15, carrying letters**: he jogs to his destination (`PhysicalLettersRoadCourierTravel`, only below stage 20).
   - **Arriving** within 1,024 units of his destination's map marker (`ArriveInTown`), **in business hours** (8:00 to 20:00, the global `GameHour`, so never into a bedroom at night): if the recipient of one of his letters can be found alive, he goes to them **wherever they are**, through doors into interiors (stage 17, a package of its own, `PhysicalLettersRoadCourierDeliver`: re-aiming the jog he had finished at the marker didn't restart it). Once both are loaded and he's within 1,500 units, **the town courier's scene plays** (`PhysicalLettersRoadDeliveryScene`, a copy of the town one in this quest: a scene plays only its own quest's topics): he runs up, stays put with them, says his four lines, and "I have a letter here for you." hands it over (`PhysicalLetters_TIF_RoadDeliver` → `DeliverNow` → `DeliverToRecipient`: delivered, then read); the recipient thanks him (the town's per-voice thanks, copied). A scene cut short hands it over silently. If he can't reach them in 3 minutes (a locked house), they can't be found, or it's out of hours, his letters go on, due now (`Transit::RoadOnward`, `GiveUpDelivery`): delivered unseen. Either way he then walks on (stage 25). Indoors is fine here, unlike the town courier's errands: the player is following him, he isn't coming to them. Earlier versions: he stood a few steps from Adrianne (the finished package), then handed it over without a word (the line said by script with `Say`, which played nothing). Tested on AE (2026-10-01): the scene plays and the letter is delivered.
   - **20, handed his letters to the player** (threatened, or beaten in a brawl): he rests where he is for 30 seconds (`PhysicalLettersRoadCourierRest`, vanilla's stay-put data), then
   - **25, walks on** (`PhysicalLettersRoadCourierWalkOn`): no letters, no hurry.
   - **Losing a brawl**: a script on his alias (`PhysicalLetters_RoadCourierAlias`) catches his bleedout while vanilla's brawl quest (`DGIntimidateQuest`) runs: armed combat doesn't count. The quest goes to stage 20 at once, so when he gets up his package is the rest, not the jog; once he's out of combat and bleedout he says "Whatever you say!" (a script-said topic, shared `0x0E0CBE`) and hands his letters over. Vanilla's brawl otherwise runs as is. **Only its yield line is his own**: vanilla's generic one is for a friendly bet ("You're tougher than you look. You got me, fair and square."), so our info in vanilla's `DGIntimidateVictoryTopic` (`0x886`, the courier only, otherwise a copy of the generic info `0x047ADB`: its script, links and walk-away topic) has him say "Don't hurt me! You win." (shared `0x0E0CBF`). Its previous info is `0x0F07B9`: in `Skyrim.esm` the topic's infos run character-specific first, then the three generic yields (`0x047ADB`, `0x078F76`, `0x047ADC`) after `0x0F07B9` (read from the plugin's record order), so ours comes just before them. Without that link it landed after them and vanilla's line won. Tested on AE (2026-10-01): he says it. A version that also skipped vanilla's yield conversation (moving the brawl to its "won" stage) broke the brawl: he fled after the first line and walked off without handing anything over.
5. **The end.** Every 5 seconds the quest checks him (not mid-fight): once he's unloaded for 30 seconds in a row (the player has time to follow him through a door), or farther from the player than where he started plus 4,096 units (not while he goes to a recipient: across cells distances mean nothing) (at least 12,288: vanilla's travel markers can be 10,000 units out, and the first test ended at once with a plain 8,192) the encounter ends (`EncounterEnded`, with the reason in the log): letters he still has go back to their state and travel on, due as before; letters he hasn't were taken by the player. He's moved back to his marker, the trigger is re-armed, the quest stops. After a load the encounter is over for the DLL (`IsLive`): `Transit::Tick` takes the letters back the same way, and the quest's next check ends it.

While he's on the road, the player's own mail is held, as during a town errand.

## Dialogue

All on him while the quest is at stage 10 (carrying letters); all vanilla shared lines, voiced for his voice type (MaleYoungEager, checked in `Skyrim - Voices_en0.bsa`).

- **Posting a letter**: the postage topic ([DELIVERY.md](DELIVERY.md#the-hand-over)) already includes the courier; the letter travels from where he is.
- **"Give me the letters you're carrying. (Intimidate)"**, a tree (vanilla's generic intimidation pattern, `DialogueFavorGenericIntimidateBranchTopic`, with a refusal step):
  - **He folds** when the player has `IntimidateSpeech` Speech (40; the global `PhysicalLettersRoadIntimidateSpeech`, set from the INI) **or** the Intimidation perk (`0x105F29`), **and** the engine's `GetIntimidateSuccess` passes: one of "No need to get rough!", "Whatever you say!", "Don't hurt me! You win." at random; the script calls vanilla's `FavorDialogueScript.Intimidate` (Speech experience, the intimidation stat) and he gives the player his letters (`GiveLetters`: `HandOverLetters`, then `RemoveItem` to the player, so the vanilla "added" messages show). Stage 20: no more dialogue.
  - **Otherwise he refuses**: "Nah. I don't think so." (stage 15: the threat isn't offered again) and the next choice is **"Then I'll take them. (Brawl)"**, also offered on its own whenever he's at stage 15: "I'll teach you to talk to me that way!" and vanilla's `FavorDialogueScript.Brawl` starts its brawl (`DGIntimidateQuest`: fists only, no crime). Winning it, vanilla plays his yield and marks him intimidated by the player.
- **"Your letters. Now."**, only while he's intimidated by the player (after a brawl won, at stage 10 or 15), for when the player is quicker than the script: "Whatever you say!" and he gives them.
- **Robbery is a crime.** Handing his letters over under threat or after a brawl, he reports it once he's away: when he despawns (the encounter ends, he's moved back to his holding cell), a non-violent bounty of `RobberyBounty` (40 gold, vanilla's assault bounty) in the hold where it happened. The hold is the road trigger's own (`WETriggerScript.myHoldLocation`, sent as the story event's location); `CrimeFactionFor` maps the nine holds to their crime factions in Skyrim.esm. The brawl itself stays crime-free, as in vanilla. The script's flag is saved with the quest, so a load before he despawns doesn't lose the report.
- **Pickpocketing** works as for anyone: the letters are in his inventory. He carries nothing else (his outfit is worn), and there is no armed-combat path: hitting him with a weapon is assault, and nothing more.

**Letters the player takes**, by any of these: a letter to the player is delivered to them (early); any other is lost to delivery: a letter between NPCs ends its thread ([NPC_TO_NPC.md](NPC_TO_NPC.md#keeping-it-bounded)); the player's own is simply theirs again (it can be posted again).

**Why the Speech requirement.** `GetIntimidateSuccess` is the engine's check (UESP, Skyrim:Speech): the player's level × (1 + (player's Speech − NPC's Speech) / 100) ^ `fIntimidateSpeechcraftCurve` against the NPC's level × a game setting for its confidence; the Intimidation perk doubles the player's. The vanilla courier is level 1 with default confidence, so on its own the threat always works (first test, 2026-10-01: intimidated at once, no brawl option). He should have some professional integrity, so the threat also needs the Speech or the perk.

## Saves

`LTRN` version 6 adds each parcel's route (world `uint32`, from and to as four `float`s, set-out time `double`) and states 5 and 6 ([PERSISTENCE.md](PERSISTENCE.md)). The co-save record `LROD` (version 1) holds the last encounter's time (`double`, game days; -1 none) for the cooldown. Parcels from older saves have no route: they aren't met on the road.

## Testing

First run on AE (2026-10-01): a letter Whiterun→Riverwood passed the player, a road trigger brought the encounter (our node ran before vanilla's random ones), he took the letter, and the encounter ended at the first check (the old 8,192 limit); not seen. Later runs found no letter passing (a timing check, then a town check, both removed). Then (2026-10-01): met him walking towards Whiterun with a letter Riverwood→Whiterun; posting a letter with him worked; the threat worked and he handed the letter over. To meet him: from Whiterun, post a letter to someone in Riverwood (or F6 and F7 looking at them), then walk the road towards Riverwood straight away; with debug logging, `[RoadCourier] A letter passes the player` says when a road trigger would bring him. Things to watch: that our node runs before vanilla's random road encounters, that he stays on the road, and how vanilla's brawl ends with an essential opponent.
