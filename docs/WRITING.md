# Writing letters

The player writes letters in the book menu through **Ink & Quill - Writing Framework** (its own repo; its `docs/API.md` and `docs/API_DESIGN.md`), which owns the editor, `book.swf`, the writing materials (quill, ink, blood), their costs, and the close prompt (Save / Discard / Keep writing). Physical Letters is its client: it owns the parchment, the letter's text and recipient, and storing letters in LetterDB. Code: `src/Writing.cpp` (the client), `Letters::Reading` and `Letters::Marked` (the text), `include/InkAndQuillAPI.h` (Ink & Quill's header, copied).

**Status:** working on AE ([Testing](#testing)).

## Parchment

The blank letter. A book item, `PhysicalLettersParchment` (`0x8B6`), with the vanilla note model (`Clutter\Books\Note01.nif`), empty, value 2, weight 0.1. Every letter copies its look from it ([PERSISTENCE.md](PERSISTENCE.md)), so a written letter looks like the parchment. Code: `src/Parchment.cpp`.

- **Crafted** at a tanning rack: 1 Roll of Paper (Skyrim.esm `0x033761`) makes 3 parchment (`PhysicalLettersRecipeParchment`, `0x8B7`).
- **Always sold** by general-goods merchants: 3 or 5 parchment after every restock (every 48 game hours). At `kDataLoaded` `Parchment::AddToMerchants` goes through the vendor factions, takes each one's merchant chest (`vendorData.merchantContainer`), and gives the chest's base record the leveled list `PhysicalLettersLItemParchment` (`0x8B8`: 3 or 5 parchment) as its own entry, **in memory**, as Ink & Quill does with its quill and inkwell. A general store is a chest that already stocks Skyrim.esm's `LItemMiscVendorMiscItems75` (`0x09AF0A`, the list that sells the Roll of Paper); blacksmiths, apothecaries and the like are left alone. Vanilla's generic world containers stock that list too (`Cupboard01`, `PersonalChestSmall`, Dragonborn's `PersonalChestSmall_NoRespawn`, hundreds placed in houses), so a merchant whose chest is one of them is skipped, with a warning; the log lists each stocked chest. Ink & Quill's `WritingTools.cpp` has the same walk for its quill and inkwell, and Physical Diaries' `BlankJournals.cpp` for its blank journals: fix all three. **No extra data on the entry.** CommonLib's `TESContainer::AddObjectToContainer` gives every new entry extra data (`ContainerItemExtra`, a COED) with health 100.0 (a plugin's is 1.0 for 100%), and the game's `InventoryChanges::InitLeveledItems` (SE 15889, AE 16129, the same in VR) has a bug: for a leveled entry whose health isn't 1.0 it sets the rolled items' health at the entry's position in the chest instead of at each item's, writing past the end of a small scratch array. That corrupts the game's scratch heap: a user's game crashed when Niranye's chest in Windhelm loaded (AE, 2026-10-19), and every stocked chest wrote out of bounds. So the entry's extra data is deleted right after it's added (`Parchment::AddToChest`), and the broken branch never runs. No vanilla record is overridden, so no patch is needed. Until 2026-10-09 the list was one more pick in that shared misc list, which gave a merchant like Belethor about a 1 in 5 chance per restock.
- **Why not the roll of paper itself:** it's a misc item, which the game can't read or use, and converting it would break other mods' recipes and scripts that count it.

## Starting

`Writing::Connect` finds Ink & Quill's API at `kPostLoad`: the header's version, 2 (the [DM Letter](DM_LETTER.md)'s free writing); an older Ink & Quill counts as not installed, "too old" in the log (user, 2026-10-31: no fallback). `Writing::Register` (at `kDataLoaded`, only if its writing is on) registers parchment as a blank (`RegisterBlank`) and adds an owner (`AddOwner`). Without Ink & Quill, or with its writing off (`Writing::Available`), letters can't be written or edited; the log says which. Parchment is then kept out of the game (user, 2026-10-04: an empty note nobody can use is junk): `Parchment::OnDataLoaded` doesn't add it to the merchants' list and clears its recipe's bench keyword (`0x8B7`), so no tanning rack lists it. Both are in memory only, every startup, so installing Ink & Quill later brings it back. Parchment the player already carries stays an empty note. Everything else (NPCs writing to the player and to each other, delivery, reading, handing letters over) works without it.

- **A new letter:** reading parchment from the player's own inventory (Ink & Quill checks where) begins a session, the caret after "To: ". Nothing is made until the first save, which turns one parchment into the letter (`ReplySaveAsBook`).
- **How it's written** is the player's choice when there's one, in Physical Letters' own message box (`OnParchmentOpen`; Ink & Quill doesn't prompt for its clients). The chosen way goes to Ink & Quill as `IQ_Session::way`, which then checks only that way's materials. The box offers:
  - **Special Ink**, when the player carries the Quill of Gemination and Special Ink (`HasQuillOfGemination`, `HasSpecialInk`).
  - **Ink**, when the player has ink or Ink & Quill doesn't need it.
  - **Blood** in Ink's place, when the player has no ink where Ink & Quill needs it (`WouldBeInBlood`: its materials are required, the player has a quill and no ink), blood is allowed (`[Writing] Blood`, [SETTINGS.md](SETTINGS.md), on by default) and the player can afford it (`CanBleed`). With no ink and no blood, neither is offered.
  - **Cancel**, also Escape.

  With Ink as the only way, writing begins at once, with no box. With nothing usable it begins in ink or blood, and Ink & Quill shows its notice (no quill, no ink, too weak). The answer comes after `onOpen` has returned; the session still counts as the parchment's, since Ink & Quill ties a session begun while a blank is open to it (Ink & Quill 0.2). It begins only if that parchment is still open. The edit key on the parchment while a message box is open does nothing, so no second box opens. Special Ink costs a bottle per save; a forgery written in it always passes for the named person's hand ([FORGERY.md](FORGERY.md#the-roll)), and any other letter in it is an ordinary letter.
- **Editing:** the edit key on a letter the player wrote and carries (so not sent) begins a session on its text. Anyone else's letter, or one read from a container or the world, isn't ours: the key does nothing.
- **Not ready:** before the session is ready (SkyrimNet and LetterDB, [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md)) parchment shows a HUD notice and doesn't open for writing.

Ink & Quill checks the chosen way's materials, and charges ink, blood or Special Ink and the parchment only when a save is accepted; discarding costs nothing. Editing a letter leaves the choice to Ink & Quill (`IQ_WRITE_ASK`): it uses ink if the player has some. Otherwise it asks about blood, or, with `[Writing] Blood` off (`noBlood`), shows its "no ink" notice.

## The text

A player's letter opens with a "From:" line (`Strings::FromLabel`) and a "To:" line (`Strings::ToLabel`), in its reading text too, so the editor and the page match. Letters to the player and between NPCs have neither. "From:" is the player's name on a new letter, and theirs to change: another name makes the letter a forgery or a signed letter ([FORGERY.md](FORGERY.md#the-from-line)). Letters written before the line (2026-10-13) show it with their author's name, the player's.

The label is in the chosen language ("An:" in German, "宛先：" in Japanese), from the locale files ([LOCALIZATION.md](LOCALIZATION.md)). It isn't stored: a letter shows the current language's.

Marked text (Ink & Quill's: what the player can't change is between U+E002 and U+E003), in the same handwriting font tags as the reading text:

```
<font face='$HandwrittenFont' size='14'>[From: ]<sender>[\nTo: ]<name, address>[\n\n</font>]<body paragraphs>[]
<font face='$HandwrittenFont' size='14'>[From: ]<sender>[\nTo: ]<name, address>[\n\n</font><body paragraphs>]      (the body locked)
```

The size is `[General] FontSize` (14 by default, [SETTINGS.md](SETTINGS.md)), on every font tag, and Ink & Quill gets the same face and size for typed text (`runFont`, `runSize`), so a new paragraph has the size the rendered page gives it.

`<body paragraphs>` is the same for every letter (the player's, NPCs', between NPCs). **Reading text:** the whole body is one `<font …>` tag, and every blank line in it holds a non-breaking space (`&nbsp;`; a line with only blood's tags counts as blank). The book menu finds each line by its first character, so an empty line never starts a new page: a letter whose overflow was blank lines read as one page, and page 2's leading blank lines vanished. With no empty line left, nothing resets the font, so one tag is enough. This is the game's own layout, used with or without Ink & Quill (2026-10-05). **Marked text:** each paragraph is `<font …>text</font>` and each `\n\n` between paragraphs is `<font …>\n\n</font>` (the editor's blank lines stay empty, and Skyrim resets the font after an empty line); an empty paragraph has no tag of its own. Every break is in the handwriting at the letter's size, so a blank line is as tall as a written one. Ink & Quill gives typed text, breaks included, the session's `runFont` and `runSize`, which are the same face and size, so the page doesn't move when a letter is saved or edited again (2026-10-04/05, AE; SNPD renders journals the same way). This needs Ink & Quill's break sizing from those values (uncommitted in Ink & Quill on 2026-10-05): with its older rule, typed breaks get the page's size and the text jumps on saving. Earlier here, breaks outside the tags were drawn in the page's size too, with the same jump.

`[…]` is locked. Run 0 is the sender's name (the "From:" line), run 1 the recipient's, run 2 the body. A begun-in-blood letter's two lines are both red. Enter keeps the caret in its run, so a player who types the name, presses Enter and writes on has written everything in run 1: on save, run 1's first line is the name and any further lines begin the body (tested on AE: the body run stayed empty and the save was refused, before this). The empty lock at the end keeps an empty body a run (Ink & Quill makes text after the last lock a run only if there is any). The reading text is the same without the markers.

**The body is locked while the "To:" line names nobody:** a new letter begins with everything after the name locked (`Letters::Marked(letter, true)`: two runs, the "From:" and "To:" lines), so the lines can't be left. At every change to it (`onChange`), `Recipients::Resolve` decides: naming one person (the full name and address) reloads the editor with the body as a run again (`from` 0, 1 and, for an edit, `-2 - 2`: Ink & Quill counts it as saved with its text when the session began, so a body reopened unchanged is no change, no close prompt and no ink; a new letter's body had no run then, so -1), and naming nobody locks it again, its text kept (`g_lockedBody`) and shown, given back when it opens. The caret stays where it was. Applies to edits too (their name is valid from the start, so they begin open). The line is one line: a line break typed or pasted into it is taken out in the same `onChange`, before it's drawn, and Enter on a line that names someone moves the caret to the start of the body (on one that doesn't, it does nothing). The "From:" line is one line too; Enter on it moves the caret to the end of the "To:" line, and it doesn't lock anything. Before this, Enter added lines to the "To:" run, so a letter could be written there with the body locked. (First built so the body stayed open once opened; changed after testing on AE, 2026-10-03: clearing the name left it open.)

**Blood:** text written in blood comes back between U+E000 and U+E001. `Letter::body` (and LetterDB's `body`) is the text without them, which is what prompts, narrations and memories read; `blood` keeps the marked body when any of it is in blood, and the book shows that part red (`#2B0202`, Ink & Quill's colour). **A letter begun in blood** has red "From:" and "To:" lines (labels, names and address), as Physical Diaries' headings: decided when a parchment's session starts (Blood chosen in its box), kept with the letter (`Letter::bloodHeading`, LetterDB's `blood_heading`), so it stays red when read and edited. Its reader is told: the reading prompts get `letter.blood` ("all" or "part") and the passages, the reading's memory and the hand-over narration say so ([READING.md](READING.md#the-prompt)).

## The recipient

Code: `src/Recipients.cpp`. The "To:" line's first line is a name, optionally followed by a comma and an address: "Karita" or "Karita, 6391 Dawnstar".

**Who a name stands for.** Two sources, merged by actor within each name (`Recipients::ActorsStarting`):

1. **People SkyrimNet has registered (the player has been near them), under the name SkyrimNet stored for them** (`AddRegistered`): `PublicSearchActors` with the typed text (500 at most), keeping the names that start with it. SkyrimNet registers actors near the player and stores their display name then, so a name another mod gives (NPCs Names Distributor's guard "Rorik Thelmsen [Whiterun Guard]") is found once the player has met them, wherever they are now. The search reads SkyrimNet's records only: no game function is asked a name and nobody is registered. Each result is used only if its UUID maps back to the same FormID (`PublicUUIDToFormID`, a lookup that registers nobody), and only if it's a person, checked from its record's own data (`IsRegisteredPerson`: not the player, deleted, disabled or dead; `Actors::IsPerson`). A runtime form's ID (`FF…`) is skipped here: the search gives the ID SkyrimNet last saw, and the game reuses those. Its UUID and name are kept for the session, so addressing it asks SkyrimNet nothing more. A SkyrimNet without `PublicSearchActors` skips this source: names come from the form map alone.
2. **Every person in memory, under their own name** (the form map: persistent references anywhere, the rest only while their cell is loaded): checked from the record's own data first (`IsNamedPerson`: not the player, deleted or disabled; a base `TESNPC` with a name of its own; `Actors::IsPerson`), then `IsDead`, then their own name (`ExtraDataList::GetDisplayName`, what `GetDisplayFullName` returns in vanilla). This is how an NPC SkyrimNet hasn't met yet is written to; one it has met is found by either name.

**A person** (`Actors::IsPerson`, shared with NPC letters and letters between NPCs): a voice type, and a race with `ActorTypeNPC` or a playable one (a mod's own race may lack the keyword; in vanilla only the ten player races are playable, all with it). No creatures: a letter to a dog or a troll isn't a thing (user, 2026-10-12). SkyrimNet's own speech rule (`AllowPcDialog`) isn't used: in the vanilla masters 20 races have it without `ActorTypeNPC`, nearly all creatures (dogs, huskies, armored trolls, the Vampire Lord, werewolves, rieklings), Miraak's the only person among them.

Names match ASCII case-insensitive. Then:

- **`[Writing] GenericRecipients`** ([SETTINGS.md](SETTINGS.md)): unique NPCs always, and only them at 0 (the default); generic ones too at 1 if SkyrimNet has memories of them (`SkyrimNet::HasMemories`, one short query each, kept for the session), or all at 2. Most NPCs another mod renames are generic: NND's guard is written to at 2, or at 1 once his whole name is typed (it isn't suggested before).
- **One reference per unique NPC:** several references of one base are the same person; the one kept is loaded, then persistent, then the lowest FormID.
- **Disabled actors never count** (user, 2026-10-03): they're mostly disabled for a reason (cut content, quest doubles, a replacer's original, someone not in the world yet), and allowing them caused more trouble than it solved. Lydia, disabled in Breezehome until the player is Thane, becomes addressable when the game enables her.

**Why no display names from the game.** `GetDisplayFullName` is hooked by other mods: NPCs Names Distributor names an actor it hasn't seen when asked, and with its obscurity an NPC the player hasn't met is shown as "[sex] [race]" ("Male Nord" for Farkas), so his display name isn't "Farkas" until he's met. The scan used to ask every actor in memory, under the form map's lock, before any check: on a new save one key sent NND 2,082 actors it hadn't seen, among them 177 Hearthfire planters (named NPCs of a Nord race, without a voice), wolves, draugr and 79 nameless records (measured with NND's debug log, SE, 2026-10-11). A user's game froze and closed on the first letter typed (1.5.97, with NND); not reading display names in the scan fixed it there (1.0.2), and it wasn't reproduced elsewhere, so the cause isn't known. The lock is held only while the actors are collected. With `DebugLog` on, each change logs what SkyrimNet's search returned and kept (and why the rest was skipped) and how many the form map added.

**Addresses.** Every person of a name gets one: `<number> <place>`.

- **The number** is the end of their SkyrimNet UUID: 4 digits, more when two of the name's people share their last 4. It's their real SkyrimNet identity (UUIDs are deterministic: the same actor has the same UUID in every save), so it can be matched against what SkyrimNet shows. Getting it registers the actor with SkyrimNet (`PublicFormIDToUUID`), so only a complete name's people are registered, never those of a prefix.
- **The place** is where they belong: their reference's persistent location (`Actor::GetEditorLocation1`, e.g. Lydia's is `WhiterunLocation`), else where they are now; named by the location just below its hold (the town: Breezehome is "Whiterun"), or the hold itself. A location that isn't under a hold (`LocTypeHold`, `0x016771`), such as the holding cells quest NPCs start in (`HoldingCell`, `0x10D4BA`, has no name, parent or keywords), or that has no name, gives "Tamriel" (`Strings::kNoPlace`): nothing reliably says which land it is in.
- **Order:** the people SkyrimNet has memories of first (the one you know is the one you mean), then by FormID.

**Resolving** (`Recipients::Resolve`, on save, and at every change to the line for the body's lock): only a whole "Name, address" names someone (ASCII case-insensitive), whether the name is shared or not: a suggestion fills the address in (Tab picks among several, Right accepts). A name alone, or part of an address, doesn't (user, 2026-10-03: a recipient is locked in only once the full name and matching address are written). Otherwise the save is refused with a message: nobody of that name; one person (their address, to write after the name); several (listing their addresses, with an example); or an address none of them has (listing them).

**An edit's recipient** is the stored letter's while its "To:" line reads as it was loaded (`Writing::ResolveLine`): the same UUID, name and address, with no search, so a letter whose recipient the game or SkyrimNet now names differently (a quest NPC renamed after SkyrimNet registered them) can still be edited and saved. A changed line is searched as any other. The UUID is what decides who gets the letter.

**More than 10 people of one name** (generic names at `GenericRecipients` 2) aren't addressed: the save says there are too many to address one of them, and the name isn't suggested.

The letter stores the address it was saved with (LetterDB's `address`) and shows "To: Karita, 6391 Dawnstar" when read; letters from before addresses show the name alone. `PublicFormIDToUUID` has registered the recipient by then, and the stored name is SkyrimNet's.

## The name as you type

Ink & Quill's suggestions (its `Suggest`, docs/API.md › Suggestions) show what could follow on the "From:" and "To:" lines, faded after the caret, one at a time: **Tab** and **Shift+Tab** cycle, **Right** accepts (typed as if by the player), **Escape** dismisses. Ink & Quill draws them and owns the keys; Physical Letters only gives the candidates. The player can still type everything.

- **When:** `onChange` (Ink & Quill tells a session the player changed a run, in the key's own UI task, with the caret's offset). For run 0 or 1 on one line with the caret at its end, `Recipients::Completions` gives the candidates and `Suggest` shows them with the keystroke; anything else clears them.
- **The candidates,** from 2 letters on:
  - an incomplete name: the rest of each name with people that starts with what's typed ("Lyd": "ia"; a trailing space counts: "Aela ": "the Huntress");
  - a complete name: ", " and each of its people's addresses, the people SkyrimNet knows first (", 6391 Dawnstar", then ", 2207 Throat of the World"), ahead of any longer names that start the same;
  - after the comma: the rest of each address that starts with what's typed.

  At most 10. A complete name registers its people with SkyrimNet for their numbers.
- **Accepting** types the completion, so `onChange` fires again: accepting "ia" offers the address next.
- **Long completions** aren't wrapped: one at the end of a line is clipped at the page edge (Ink & Quill's overlay).
- **Cost:** each change to run 0 or 1 searches SkyrimNet's actors (up to 500, a read of its records) and scans the form map once (`Recipients::NewText`, then `Resolve` for the body's lock and `Completions` filter that result); SkyrimNet's answers (UUIDs, names, memories) and the places are kept per actor for the session. SkyrimNet is asked about memories only for a complete name's people, so at `GenericRecipients` 1 a generic NPC's name is suggested once it has been typed in full in that session, never while it's being typed. Changes to the body cost nothing.
- **History:** before `Suggest`, the preview was locked faded text after the name, replaced with `Reload` (first from a watcher polling every 150 ms, then 16 ms, then from `onChange`); it couldn't cycle, and typed text took the faded colour from text before it.

## Saving

`OnSave` gets the runs (two while a new letter's body is still locked). Refused with a message (writing goes on, nothing is charged), in this order: the session isn't ready; no name ("The letter isn't addressed yet…"); no single recipient (the reasons in [The recipient](#the-recipient)); a "From:" line refused ([FORGERY.md](FORGERY.md#the-from-line)); an empty body; a failure (logged). The recipient comes before the body, so a letter with a wrong name says so rather than that nothing is written. The body is stored as written, trimmed only at the end: blank lines or spaces before the first paragraph are kept (user, 2026-10-04; until then a full trim dropped them). A body of nothing but blank lines counts as empty. How its blank lines are rendered: [The text](#the-text).

- **Parchment:** a new letter (`Letters::Create`), put in the player's inventory; `ReplySaveAsBook` has Ink & Quill take one parchment and show the letter in the open menu.
- **An edit:** a new LetterDB record (a new letter id) that the item now holds (`Letters::Rewrite`: its DynamicForms key, name, text, item card); the old record stays, so other saves of the character keep their version. A "return to sender" mark goes with the old record. If only the names' spelling changed (the same recipient, address, sender, body and blood), nothing new is stored.
- Either way the letter is the player's (`authorUuid` theirs), written now, and is posted or handed over as any other ([DELIVERY.md](DELIVERY.md), [HAND_IN.md](HAND_IN.md)).

A save ends the session (Ink & Quill's rule): writing again is the edit key.
